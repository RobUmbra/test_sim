\textbf{Problem 1}

The sum of an infinite geometric series is $120$. The sum of the squares of its terms is $4800$. Find the first term of the original series.

\textbf{Problem 2}

Find the number of positive divisors of $10!$ that are perfect squares.

\textbf{Problem 3}

How many three-letter strings can be formed from the letters of the word "AIME" (with replacement allowed) such that no two adjacent letters are identical?

\textbf{Problem 4}

A right triangle has legs of length $150$ and $200$. Find the radius of its inscribed circle.

\textbf{Problem 5}

Let $x$ and $y$ be positive real numbers such that $x+y=10$ and $x^2 y^2 = 16$. Find the value of $x^3 + y^3$.

\textbf{Problem 6}

Find the number of paths from $(0,0)$ to $(5,5)$ on a Cartesian coordinate grid, moving only exactly one unit right or one unit up at a time, that do not pass through the point $(2,2)$.

\textbf{Problem 7}

The roots of the equation $x^2 - 17x + k = 0$ are both positive integers. Find the number of possible positive integer values for $k$.

\textbf{Problem 8}

Find the remainder when $2^{2026}$ is divided by $13$.

\textbf{Problem 9}

Find the sum of all positive integers $n$ such that $n^2 + 6n + 40$ is a perfect square.

\textbf{Problem 10}

A rectangle has an area of $120$ and a perimeter of $46$. Find the length of its diagonal.

\textbf{Problem 11}

How many four-digit positive integers have the property that the sum of their digits is $4$?

\textbf{Problem 12}

Let $f(x) = ax+b$ where $a$ and $b$ are real numbers. If $f(f(f(x))) = 8x + 21$ for all real $x$, find the value of $a+b$.

\textbf{Problem 13}

Find the area of a rhombus that has a side length of $13$ and one diagonal of length $10$.

\textbf{Problem 14}

In a geometric sequence of real numbers, the sum of the first two terms is $7$ and the sum of the first six terms is $91$. Find the sum of the first four terms.

\textbf{Problem 15}

A committee of $3$ people is to be chosen randomly from a group of $5$ men and $6$ women. The probability that the committee consists of exactly $2$ women can be written as the fraction $\frac{m}{n}$ in simplest form, where $m$ and $n$ are relatively prime positive integers. Find $m+n$.

\textbf{Problem 16}

Find the number of positive integers $x$ such that $x \le 1000$ and the greatest common divisor of $x$ and $1000$ is $1$.
